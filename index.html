Early Years

Noah Lyles was born on July 18, 1997, in Gainesville, Florida, to Keisha Caine Bishop and Kevin Lyles. His parents met as students at Seton Hall University, where they both competed in track and field. He has two siblings, Josephus and Abby. Following their parents’ divorce, Lyles and his siblings relocated with their mother to Alexandria, Virginia.

During his early years, Lyles was homeschooled while growing up with asthma, but was later able to attend public school. Although he was originally a gymnast, he took up track and field at the age of 12. A defining moment occurred while watching the 2012 Summer Olympics on TV, when he and his brother declared their intention to run together at the 2016 Summer Games. He attended T. C. Williams High School (now Alexandria City High School).

Professional Years

Lyles’ professional development began to accelerate when he represented the United States at the 2014 Youth Olympic Games, winning a gold medal in the 200 m.

His transition to elite competition included several key milestones: High School Success: In January 2015, as a high school junior, he cleared 2.03 m (6 ft 7¾ in) in the high jump. By November 2015, he was named the 2015 high school boys athlete of the year by Track & Field News. 2016 Breakthroughs: In March 2016, he won the 200 m at the New Balance Nationals Indoor. In April 2016, he swept the 100 m and 200 m at the Arcadia Invitational. In June 2016, he won the 100 m in 10.08 s at the USA Junior Championships.

Olympic Trials & World U20: In July 2016, he competed at the U.S. Olympic Trials. While he did not advance in the 100 m, he won his 200 m semi-final and placed fourth in the final with a time of 20.09 s, breaking a 31-year-old national high school record. Although he narrowly missed the 2016 Olympic team, he went on to become a double gold medalist at the 2016 World U20 Championships, winning the 100 m and the 4 × 100 m relay titles.

The Rise to Global Dominance (2017–2019): At the World Championships in Doha in 2019, he captured his first major outdoor titles, winning gold in the 200 m and the 4x100 m relay. In 2022 in Eugene, Lyles defended his 200 m title in spectacular fashion, clocking 19.31 seconds and shattering Michael Johnson’s 26-year-old American record.

Achieving the "Treble" and Olympic Glory (2023–2024): In 2023 in Budapest, Lyles achieved a historic "sprint treble," winning three gold medals (100 m, 200 m, and 4x100 m relay). At the 2024 Paris Olympics, Lyles fulfilled his lifelong dream by winning the Olympic Gold medal in the 100 m in a historic photo finish.
